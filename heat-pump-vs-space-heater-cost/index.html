Heat pump vs space heater vs gas furnace: what 1 therm of heat costs

At U.S. average prices (electricity 18.19¢/kWh, gas $15.34/Mcf), 100,000 Btu of heat costs $5.33 from a space heater, $2.43 from a heat pump at the federal minimum HSPF2 of 7.5, and $1.85 from an 80% gas furnace.

General information, not financial advice. A heat pump's real efficiency depends on your climate, the unit and the installation; the rating used here is a standardized seasonal average, not a measurement of your home. Prices: EIA averages (sources below); use the prices on your own bills when you can.

Calculator

HSPF2:

The optional field turns last winter's gas into delivered heat (therms × AFUE) and prices the same heat with the other two options. Use only the heating part of your gas use: if the gas also runs a water heater or a range, subtract their share (for example the average of your summer months).

How the comparison works

  • Space heater (electric resistance): 1 kWh becomes 3,412 Btu of heat, so 1 therm (100,000 Btu) needs 29.3 kWh.
  • Heat pump: HSPF is the heat delivered over the heating season in Btu divided by the electricity used in watt-hours (10 CFR part 430, subpart B, appendix M, section 1.2). HSPF2 is the same ratio under the test procedure in force since 2023 (appendix M1). Dividing by 3.412 gives the seasonal coefficient of performance: HSPF2 7.5 ≈ COP 2.2, so 1 therm of heat needs about 13.3 kWh.
  • Federal minimums for central heat pumps made from 2023-01-01 (10 CFR 430.32(c)(5)): HSPF2 7.5 for split systems, 6.7 for single-package units, 6.3 for space-constrained products, 6.1 for small-duct high-velocity systems. Many units on sale are rated higher; use the number for your model.
  • Gas furnace: 1 cubic foot of gas ≈ 1,036 Btu (EIA), so 1 Mcf ≈ 10.36 therms; the furnace delivers AFUE % of it. Federal minimum 80% for non-weatherized gas furnaces made from 2015-11-19 (10 CFR 430.32(e)(1)).

Where the rating misleads. The required HSPF2 rating uses the heating hours of one climate region (Region IV, appendix M1 section 5.1); the regulation treats ratings for other regions as optional. In colder places a heat pump spends more hours in cold weather, where its efficiency drops and electric backup heat (which costs the same as a space heater per Btu) may switch on. Treat the heat pump column as a best case for cold climates and ask for the cold-climate rating of a specific model.

At these averages a heat pump at HSPF2 7.5 costs less per therm than an 80% furnace in 9 of 51 jurisdictions (Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii, Louisiana, Mississippi, Texas, Washington). In every state it costs less than a space heater, by the factor COP = 2.2. Equipment, installation and maintenance costs are not included.

What this page does not include

  • Purchase and installation costs, tax credits or rebates (they change; check the program's official page).
  • Furnace blower and heat pump fan electricity beyond what the ratings include, duct losses and fixed monthly charges.
  • Cooling: a heat pump also replaces an air conditioner, which can change the decision.
