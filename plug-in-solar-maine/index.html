Plug-in solar in Maine: the 420 W rule explained

Maine's plug-in solar law (Title 35-A, section 3475) has two tiers. Up to 420 W you plug in and you are done. From 420 W to 1,200 W you need a licensed electrician, a dedicated circuit and a notice to your utility. This page covers what the statute says and how to tell which tier a kit falls in.

General information, not legal or electrical advice. Statute text checked on 2026-10-31 on legislature.maine.gov (link below). History note: P.L. 2025, c. 644 (LD 1730), signed April 6, 2026, not enacted as an emergency measure, so it took effect on the general effective date for nonemergency laws of the 132nd Legislature's Second Regular Session: July 29, 2026 (checked 2026-11-07 on legislature.maine.gov).

The short answer

A Maine retail electricity customer may install and operate an eligible plug-in solar or plug-in battery system at their service address to offset their own use. If the combined inverter AC output at that address is 420 W or less, the statute sets no further conditions. Between 420 W and 1,200 W, each system must be installed by a Maine-licensed electrician on a dedicated circuit with a single outlet, and you must notify the utility within 30 days. In both tiers the utility may not require approval, an application or a fee, and the system earns no net energy billing credit.

Which tier is your kit in?

The limit is the combined inverter output (AC) per service address, not the panels' DC rating. Read the inverter's rated AC output on its datasheet, and add up every plug-in system on the same service address.

Some inverters can be set to a lower output in software. If you rely on a setting to stay at 420 W or below, ask the seller for documentation that the certified configuration has that rating.

What counts as an "eligible system" (subsection 1)

  • A plug-in photovoltaic system or a plug-in battery system with an export capacity of 1,200 W or less.
  • Listed or certified under UL 3700 (or related UL and National Electrical Code plug-in standards), a comparable certification from a nationally recognized testing laboratory, or a National Electrical Code configuration adopted by the Electricians' Examining Board.
  • For solar: connects through a standard outlet, is mainly meant to offset the customer's own use, and has inverters that shut off within 0.2 seconds after grid power is disrupted.
  • Plug-in batteries are covered too: an AC-coupled storage device connected through a standard outlet that can charge and discharge on its own, with or without solar.

Steps in order

  1. Find the inverter's rated AC output and add up all plug-in systems at your service address.
  2. Check the certification (UL 3700 or comparable NRTL listing) for the complete system.
  3. Renters: get the landlord's written permission (see below).
  4. 420 W or less: plug it into a standard outlet. The statute requires no notice.
  5. Above 420 W, up to 1,200 W: have a Maine-licensed electrician install each system on a dedicated circuit with a single outlet, then notify your utility within 30 days of installation on the form prescribed by the Public Utilities Commission. The Commission publishes it on its Plug-In Solar page. The notice covers at least your service address, the inverter capacity and a statement that you comply with the section. Versant Power customers: Versant says to send the completed form by email to dginterconnections@versantpower.com or by mail to Versant Power c/o DG Interconnections, PO Box 932, Bangor, ME 04402 (Versant balcony solar page, checked 2026-11-07). Central Maine Power and other utilities: check your utility's website for where to send the form. A utility may not deny an installation that complies with the section.

What your utility can and cannot do (subsections 4 to 7)

  • It may not require approval before you install or operate, an interconnection application, agreement or study, any fee or charge, or controls and equipment beyond what the system already includes.
  • The system may not be used for net energy billing. Power you send to the grid earns nothing; only power you use while the sun shines lowers your bill.
  • The utility is not liable for damage or injury caused by a compliant installation. Ask whether your renter's or homeowner's insurance covers it.

Renters and buildings you do not own (subsection 8)

If you install on a structure you do not own, you must make sure the installation does not compromise the structure and does not violate state or local building, fire or zoning codes, and you must restore the structure to its prior condition when you remove the system. The section gives tenants no right to install against the landlord's wishes (unlike Virginia's tenant provision), so your lease decides: ask for written permission first. Above 420 W, the electrician and the new dedicated circuit will in practice need the owner's consent too.

Before you buy: 6 checks

  1. You know the inverter's rated AC output, and the total at your address.
  2. If you want to avoid the electrician, the total is 420 W or less.
  3. You have the UL 3700 (or comparable NRTL) certification and have looked it up on the lab's website.
  4. The datasheet states the 0.2-second shutdown on grid loss.
  5. Renters: written permission from the landlord, including for any new circuit.
  6. You have estimated payback with your own price per kWh and daytime use, counting exported power at $0. Compare a 400 W and an 800 W setup: the cheaper tier may pay back sooner once electrician costs are included.

Frequently asked questions

Is plug-in solar legal in Maine?

Yes. 35-A M.R.S. 3475 (P.L. 2025, c. 644) lets a retail electricity customer install and operate an eligible plug-in solar or plug-in battery system at their service address to offset their own use. The law took effect on July 29, 2026, the general effective date for nonemergency laws of that session.

What is the maximum wattage allowed?

An eligible system has an export capacity of 1,200 W or less. Within that, the combined inverter AC output per service address decides the tier: 420 W or less has no further conditions; above 420 W and up to 1,200 W needs a licensed electrician, a dedicated circuit and a utility notice.

Does the 420 W limit refer to the panels or the inverter?

The inverter. The statute counts the combined inverter AC output at the service address, not the panels' DC rating. Two 400 W panels on one inverter rated 400 W AC stay at 400 W; two separate 400 W AC kits at the same address add up to 800 W.

Do I need to tell my utility?

Only above 420 W. Then you must notify the utility within 30 days of installation on the form prescribed by the Maine Public Utilities Commission, giving at least your service address, the inverter capacity and a statement that you comply with the section. At 420 W or less the statute requires no notice.

Do I need an electrician?

Only above 420 W combined at the address. Then each system must be installed by a Maine-licensed electrician on a dedicated circuit with a single outlet. At 420 W or less you plug the system into a standard outlet.

Can the utility charge a fee or refuse my system?

No. The utility may not require approval, an interconnection application, agreement or study, any fee or charge, or controls and equipment beyond what the system already includes, and it may not deny an installation that complies with the section.

Will I get paid for power I send to the grid?

No. The system may not be used for net energy billing, so count exported power at $0. At the Maine average residential price of 30.23 cents/kWh (EIA, January-August 2026, preliminary), each kWh you use yourself while the panel produces is worth about 30 cents; your own per-kWh price can differ.

Can I install one if I rent?

The section gives tenants no right to install against the landlord's wishes, so your lease decides: get written permission first. If you install on a structure you do not own, you must not compromise it or violate building, fire or zoning codes, and you must restore it when you remove the system. Above 420 W the electrician and new circuit will in practice need the owner's consent.
