Plug-in solar in Virginia: what changes on January 1, 2027

Two new sections of the Code of Virginia (56-596.8 for utility customers, 55.1-1212.1 for tenants) take effect on January 1, 2027. This page covers what they say, the steps in order, and what to check before you buy.

General information, not legal or electrical advice. Statute text checked on 2026-10-31 on law.lis.virginia.gov (links below). The sections are not in force until January 1, 2027. Until then, your utility's current interconnection rules apply. Read the current text before you rely on it.

The short answer

From January 1, 2027, a Virginia utility customer may own and operate a certified plug-in ("balcony") solar device of up to 1,200 W without an interconnection agreement and without utility approval or fees. Unlike Utah, Virginia asks you to notify your utility before you install, on a form from the State Corporation Commission (SCC). Virginia also gives tenants of larger landlords a right to install one, with a 7-day notice to the landlord.

What counts as a "small portable solar generation device"

Condition in 56-596.8 AWhat to check on a kit
Maximum output of 1,200 W per customer, or per residential unit in multi-family housingThe inverter's rated output. The limit is per customer or per unit, so two kits on one account count together.
Connects to the building's electrical system through an outletA plug for a normal outlet. Hard-wired systems are outside this section.
On the customer's side of the meter, mainly to offset the customer's own useSize it to your daytime use, not to export power.
Meets the most recent National Electrical CodeAsk the seller which NEC edition the product is designed to.
Certified by a nationally recognized testing laboratory (NRTL)Ask for the listing of the complete system (lab, standard, certificate number) and look it up on the lab's website.
A feature that stops it from exporting power or affecting the building system during an outageConfirm the outage shutdown in the certification.

Devices with an output to the outlet of 391 W or less are exempt from product listing provisions that would require changes to the premises, wiring or electrical panel.

Steps in order (from January 1, 2027)

  1. Check that the device meets every condition in the table above.
  2. Renters: send the landlord the 7-day notice described below, and wait for any size, place or manner limits.
  3. Before installing, submit the SCC's notification form to your electric service provider, online or by mail.
  4. The provider has 15 days to review it and tell you about any deficiencies. If it does not respond within 15 days, you are treated as having met the notification requirement.
  5. Install. Where a premises has several devices, or the distribution circuit requires it, the utility or cooperative may install an automatic locking disconnect switch.

The section does not say who pays for that switch. Ask your utility before buying a second device. The statute refers to the SCC's form without linking it; look for it on scc.virginia.gov closer to the start date.

What your utility can and cannot do (56-596.8 B to E)

  • It may not require its approval, charge a fee, or require controls or equipment beyond what the device includes.
  • The device is outside interconnection requirements and outside net energy metering. Power you send to the grid earns nothing; only power you use while the sun shines lowers your bill.
  • Investor-owned utilities, municipal utilities and electric cooperatives are not liable for damage, injury or service interruption caused by the device. Ask whether your renter's or homeowner's insurance covers it.
  • You are responsible for making sure the device does not export power or affect the building system during an outage.

Renters (55.1-1212.1)

TopicWhat the statute says
Which landlordsLandlords who own more than four rental units, or more than a 10% interest in more than four, individually or through a business entity
Your rightThe landlord may not prohibit installation on the exterior of your premises. It may set reasonable limits on size, place and manner, and may prohibit or restrict installation elsewhere on the property
NoticeAt least 7 days before installing, with documentation that the device meets the technical requirements and the proposed location
DamageYou are responsible for damage to the unit or premises caused by the device. The landlord is not liable under the listed habitability provisions for a condition caused solely by the device
Ratio utility billing (RUBS)If your unit is billed under a ratio utility billing system, you need the landlord's express written approval to install or use a device. 56-596.8 does not apply to these units

If your landlord owns four units or fewer, the statute gives you no specific right: your lease applies, so get written permission first.

Before you buy: 6 checks

  1. The inverter's rated output is 1,200 W or less for your whole account or unit.
  2. You have the NRTL certification for the complete system and have verified it.
  3. The device shuts down during an outage (stated in the certification).
  4. Renters: you know whether your landlord owns more than four units and whether your unit is billed under RUBS.
  5. You plan to file the SCC notice before installing, on or after January 1, 2027.
  6. You have estimated payback with your own price per kWh and daytime use, counting exported power at $0.
