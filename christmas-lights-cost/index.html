How much do Christmas lights cost to run?

Cost = watts × hours ÷ 1,000 × price per kWh. At the U.S. average residential price (18.19¢/kWh), lights on 6 hours a day for 40 days cost $4.37 per 100 W, $21.83 for a 500 W display and $65.48 for 1,500 W.

General information. The result is only as good as the wattage you enter: use the rating printed on the box, the tag near the plug or the product page, and the price per kWh on your own bill when you can. State prices are EIA averages (sources below).

How to read the numbers

  • Watts: the power a set draws while lit. 1,000 W on for 1 hour = 1 kWh, the unit on your bill.
  • Price per kWh: divide the energy charges on your bill (supply + delivery) by the kWh used, or see how to find your price per kWh. Fixed monthly charges do not change when the lights are on, so leave them out.
  • Timer: the hours per day are what you pay for. Going from 12 to 6 hours halves the cost; the calculator shows what each extra hour per day adds over the season.
  • Old vs new sets: enter each set's rated watts in its own row and compare by setting "how many" to 0 for one of them. LED sets usually list far fewer watts than incandescent sets of similar length; use the actual ratings rather than a rule of thumb.

Safety comes from the product's own instructions: follow its indoor/outdoor marking and, if the label states a maximum number of sets connected end to end, do not exceed it.
