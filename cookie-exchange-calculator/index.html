Cookie exchange calculator

How many cookies each baker needs for a holiday cookie swap, how many batches that is with your recipe, and a sign-up list that catches two people bringing the same cookie and prints a label for each plate. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is saved or sent.

How many cookies do I bake?

How it is calculated: (bakers, or bakers minus 1 if you do not keep your own) × cookies per person + tasting cookies, then + buffer %, rounded up. Batches = that total ÷ your recipe's yield, rounded up. "A dozen per person" is a common house rule, not a standard: agree on it in the invite.

Hosting checklist

  1. In the invite: the number of cookies per person, the date, and a deadline to tell you which cookie each baker is bringing.
  2. About a week before: fill in the sign-up list, sort out duplicates, and send everyone the final head count so they can bake the right amount.
  3. Packaging: ask bakers to bring their cookies already split into bags or boxes of the agreed amount (one per person), or to bring empty containers so guests can fill their own.
  4. At the table: one plate and one printed label per cookie, plus a few extra cookies for tasting.
  5. Allergies: ask in the invite if anyone has a food allergy. Labels list the main allergens a baker knows are in the recipe; they do not guarantee the absence of traces.

FAQ

How many cookies should each person bring to a cookie exchange?

The most common house rule is one dozen for every participant, including yourself, so in a group of 8 each baker brings 8 dozen (96 cookies). Many hosts switch to half a dozen per person for groups above about 12, which halves the baking. Use the calculator to add cookies for tasting and a buffer for breakage.

Do I count myself when working out how many cookies to bake?

Usually yes: each baker also keeps one set of their own cookies, so everyone goes home with one set from every baker. If your group does not keep its own cookies, untick the box and you bake one set fewer.

How many batches do I need?

Divide the total by what your recipe makes and round up. The calculator does this with the yield you enter; check the yield printed on your recipe, because it depends on cookie size. Rounding up leaves spare cookies, which the result shows.

How do I scale my recipe?

Multiply every ingredient by the number of batches. Making separate batches is usually easier than one very large one, because a home mixer and oven trays have a limited capacity.

How do we avoid everyone bringing chocolate chip?

Collect each baker's cookie in the sign-up list before the party. The list highlights entries that look like the same cookie, so one of the bakers can switch.

What should go on the label at the swap table?

The cookie name, the baker and the main allergens the baker knows are in the recipe (for example nuts, peanuts, milk, eggs, wheat). Print one label per cookie from the sign-up list. Guests with allergies should still ask the baker, because a label cannot rule out cross-contact in a home kitchen.

Is anything saved or sent?

No. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is stored: reloading the page clears it. To keep the list, print it or copy it.
