Do gift cards expire? The US federal 5-year rule

Under federal law, the money on a covered US gift card cannot expire sooner than 5 years after the card was sold or last reloaded. An inactivity or service fee is allowed only after 12 months with no use, only if the card discloses it, and at most once per calendar month. A card bought on 20 December 2026: its funds are protected at least until 20 December 2031.

General information, not legal advice. Federal minimum rules for consumer gift cards sold in the United States (Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.20), read on 2026-11-07 (see Sources). Many states give more protection (for example no expiration at all): federal law does not override a state law that protects consumers more. This page does not list state rules.

The federal rules in plain English

QuestionFederal ruleSource
Which cardsGift certificates, store gift cards and general-use prepaid gift cards (network-branded cards usable at many unaffiliated merchants), sold or issued to consumers primarily for personal, family or household purposes.12 CFR 1005.20(a)(1)-(3)
When can the money expire?Not before the later of: 5 years after the date the certificate was issued or the card was last loaded with funds; or the expiration date of the card, if any.1005.20(e)(2)
The plastic expires before the moneyAllowed, but no fee may be charged to replace the card or to give you the remaining balance in another way before the funds expire, unless the card was lost or stolen.1005.20(e)(4)
What the card must sayThe expiration date of the funds (or that they do not expire), a toll-free number (and website, if any) for a replacement card, and, next to the card expiration date, a statement that the card expires but the funds do not or expire later, and that you can contact the issuer for a replacement (not required on a non-reloadable card whose expiration date is at least 7 years from manufacture).1005.20(e)(3)
Inactivity, dormancy or service feesOnly if there was no activity in the one-year period ending on the day the fee is charged; the amount, how often and the fact it is charged for inactivity are stated clearly on the card; and no more than one such fee per calendar month.1005.20(d)(1)-(3)
What counts as activityAny action that increases or decreases the money on the card (a purchase, a reload). The fee itself, error corrections and reversals do not count.1005.20(a)(7)
Other feesEvery fee must be disclosed: type, amount (or how it is calculated) and when it applies, plus a toll-free number for fee information.1005.20(f)
State lawsA state law is not inconsistent with the federal law if it gives the consumer greater protection.15 U.S.C. 1693q

Practical habit: using even part of the balance is activity, so it restarts the 12-month fee clock. It does not restart the 5-year expiration clock, which for reloadable cards runs from the last time money was added.

Cards the federal rules do not cover

Regulation 1005.20(b) excludes cards or certificates that are:

  • usable only for telephone services;
  • reloadable and not marketed or labeled as a gift card or gift certificate;
  • loyalty, award or promotional gift cards (for example "free $10 card with a $50 purchase" or rewards points): they must say on the front that they are promotional and when the funds expire, and disclose any fees, but the 5-year minimum does not apply;
  • not marketed to the general public;
  • issued in paper form only;
  • redeemable only for admission to events or venues at a particular location or group of affiliated locations (and related goods or services).

For these, read the card's terms and your state's rules.

What to do with an old card

  1. Look at the back of the card or the card holder: funds expiration date, fees and the toll-free number must be there.
  2. Check the balance on the issuer's website or toll-free number before you shop.
  3. If the plastic has expired but the funds have not, ask for a replacement: it must be free unless the card was lost or stolen.
  4. Your state may protect you more: check your state attorney general or consumer protection office.
