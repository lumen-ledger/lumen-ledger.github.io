Cost per load from the EnergyGuide label

The yellow label on a U.S. clothes washer or dishwasher gives kWh per year for a fixed number of loads a week. Divide, multiply by your price per kWh, and you get the cost of one load, then of your own year.

General information, not financial advice. Rates: U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.B, average residential price January–August 2026 (release 2026-10-23, read 2026-10-31). The label figure comes from a federal test, not from your kitchen: hot water temperature, cycle choice and load size change real use. Use the price per kWh from your bill if you have it (how to find it).

What the label says

  • Clothes washers: “Estimated energy cost is based on six wash loads a week” and a national average of 14 cents per kWh and $1.21 per therm of natural gas (16 CFR 305.15(a)(10) and Appendix K1). The label shows a yearly cost with an electric water heater and one with a natural gas water heater.
  • Dishwashers: the same statement with four wash loads a week (16 CFR 305.16(a)(9)).
  • Both labels give the model's estimated yearly energy use in kWh (16 CFR 305.15(a)(8), 305.16(a)(8)). That number is what you type below.

Calculator

Formula: kWh per load = label kWh ÷ (label loads a week × 52). Your yearly cost = kWh per load × your loads a week × 52 × your price per kWh. The natural gas line is only rescaled for your number of loads: it stays at the label's prices (14 cents per kWh, $1.21 per therm), because the label does not split the electricity and gas parts.

Worked example: an older vs a 2027 dishwasher

Federal standards cap a standard-size dishwasher at 307 kWh/year if made from May 30, 2013, and at 223 kWh/year if made from April 23, 2027 (10 CFR 430.32(f)(1) and (f)(2); the 2027 energy limit does not apply to standard models whose normal cycle takes 60 minutes or less). At the label basis of 4 loads a week (208 a year) and the U.S. average rate of 18.19 cents:

Difference: $15.28 a year, $152.80 over ten years, before any price change. The 307 kWh label itself would print $42.98 a year, because it uses 14 cents per kWh. These are the legal maximums, not specific models: most labels show less, so read your own.

Limits of this estimate

  • The label rounds the federal test's usage to whole loads a week. The Department of Energy procedures use 295 cycles a year for clothes washers (appendix J2) and 215 or 184 cycles a year for dishwashers (appendix C1 or C2) (10 CFR 430.23(c)(1), (j)(1)). If your label's kWh was computed on 184 cycles, the real kWh per load is about 13% higher than shown here; on 215 cycles, about 3% lower. For washers, 295 cycles instead of 312 means about 6% more per load.
  • Heating the water is part of a washer's measured energy, priced separately for electric and for gas or oil water heaters (10 CFR 430.23(j)(1)); that is why the label prints two yearly costs. If your water heater is gas, the kWh figure overstates your electricity: use the label's gas line.
  • Clothes dryers carry no EnergyGuide label: estimate them with watts × hours in the appliance running cost calculator.
  • Water and sewer charges are not included.
