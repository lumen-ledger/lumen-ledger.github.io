How much does it cost to charge an EV at home?

Cost = miles ÷ 100 × kWh per 100 miles × your price per kWh. At the U.S. average residential rate of 18.19 cents, a car using 30 kWh/100 mi costs $5.46 per 100 miles, or $68.21 a month for 1,250 miles.

General information, not financial advice. Rates: U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.B, average residential price January–August 2026 (release 2026-10-23, read 2026-10-31). The average includes fixed charges and taxes; your rate may differ by month, usage tier or time of day. Use the price per kWh from your bill if you have it (how to find it). Public fast charging is priced differently and is not covered here.

Where to find your car's kWh per 100 miles

  • The EPA window sticker and fueleconomy.gov show kWh/100 mi next to MPGe. Lower is better.
  • If you only have MPGe: kWh/100 mi = 3,370.5 ÷ MPGe, because EPA counts 33.705 kWh as one gallon of gasoline (40 CFR 600.002). Example: 139 MPGe (2026 Tesla Model 3 Standard RWD, fueleconomy.gov top-ten list, read 2026-11-07) ≈ 24.2 kWh/100 mi.
  • Charging loss. EPA's test procedure records the AC watt-hours drawn during recharging (40 CFR 600.116-12(c)(8)), so the label figure is measured on the wall side: leave the extra loss at 0 %. If you read consumption from the car's trip computer instead, it usually reports battery-side energy, so add a loss. The U.S. Department of Energy puts the loss from wall to battery at about 16 % (Fact of the Week #1045, 2018); fueleconomy.gov gives battery charging efficiency as often 84–93 %.
  • Cold weather, highway speed and cabin heating raise consumption; a month of your own data beats any label.

When does charging at home cost as much as gasoline?

The break-even price per kWh is gasoline cost per mile ÷ kWh per mile. With the defaults (30 mpg, $4.153/gal, 30 kWh/100 mi) it is $0.461 per kWh; the calculator shows it for your numbers. This compares energy only: it leaves out purchase price, insurance, maintenance, registration fees, and any time-of-use or EV rate your utility may offer. Gasoline prices also differ by state; enter your local price.
