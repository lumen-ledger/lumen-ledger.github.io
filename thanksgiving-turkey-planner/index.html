Turkey planner: when to start thawing, when it goes in the oven

Weight + dinner time → thaw start, oven time at 325 °F, first thermometer check. Example: a 16 lb turkey for a 3:00 pm dinner on Thursday, November 26, 2026 goes in the fridge to thaw by Saturday, November 21 and in the oven by 10:25 AM.

General information, not food-safety advice for your situation. Times are approximate: the turkey is done only when a food thermometer reads 165 °F in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost part of the thigh and the wing. Tables from University of Georgia Extension Circular 1226 (reviewed 2026-09-15), which is based on USDA FSIS guidance, read 2026-10-31. The USDA and FoodSafety.gov pages refused automated reading that day, so check them before cooking: USDA FSIS “Let's Talk Turkey”.

How the plan is built: the turkey comes out of the oven 20 minutes before dinner to rest. It goes in early enough for the longest roasting time in its weight row, and you start checking the temperature at the shortest time. If it reaches 165 °F early, cover it with foil while it waits. Thawing starts early enough for the longest time in the thawing row. When a weight sits on the border of two rows (for example 12 or 16 lb), the planner uses the shortest and longest times across both rows.

  • Refrigerator: the safest method. Keep the turkey in its wrapper on a tray on the bottom shelf. Starting a day early is fine.
  • Cold water: keep the turkey in a leak-proof bag, submerged in cold tap water. Change the water every 30 minutes, and cook it right after it thaws. A 20 lb turkey means 10–12 hours of water changes.
  • From frozen: it is safe, but it takes at least 50% longer. Remove the giblet bag with tongs once the turkey has partly thawed in the oven. The planner multiplies the time by 1.5, which is a minimum.
  • Never thaw a turkey on the counter: its surface stays in the 40–140 °F danger zone, where bacteria multiply fast.
  • Bake the stuffing in a separate dish until it reaches 165 °F in the center.

Microwave thawing: about 6 minutes per pound on the weight-based defrost setting. Follow your microwave's manual and cook the turkey immediately afterwards.

After dinner

  • Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours (1 hour above 90 °F), in shallow containers.
  • Use turkey, stuffing and gravy within 3–4 days, or freeze them (best within 2–6 months).
  • Reheat leftovers to 165 °F.
