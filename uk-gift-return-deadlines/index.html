UK gift returns: how long you have

For a gift bought online, the 14-day cancellation period runs from delivery, not from Christmas Day. Delivered on 10 December: cancel by 24 December. Delivered on 23 December: cancel by 6 January. In a shop, there is no legal right to return something just because you changed your mind: that is store policy.

General information, not legal advice. Covers consumer purchases from a business in the UK. Legislation read on legislation.gov.uk on 2026-11-07 (see Sources). For a specific dispute, ask the retailer first, then a consumer advice service.

Gifts: these are rights under the contract between the retailer and the buyer. If you received the gift, the return is usually started by whoever bought it (account, order number, refund to their card). Gift receipts and the longer Christmas return windows many retailers advertise are voluntary store policies: read the policy on the receipt or website, it can be more generous than the law.

What cannot be cancelled for a change of mind

Regulation 28 excludes or ends the right for, among others:

  • goods made to your specifications or clearly personalised (engraved, printed with a photo);
  • goods liable to deteriorate or expire rapidly (fresh food, flowers);
  • sealed goods not suitable for return for health or hygiene reasons, once unsealed after delivery;
  • sealed audio or video recordings and sealed software, once unsealed after delivery;
  • goods that, after delivery, are inseparably mixed with other items;
  • newspapers and magazines (except subscriptions);
  • leisure, accommodation or catering services for a specific date (a ticket for a show on a set day).

If the gift is faulty: Consumer Rights Act 2015

These rights apply to shop purchases too, and have nothing to do with changing your mind:

  • Short-term right to reject: 30 days for a full refund, counted from the first day after ownership or possession has passed, the goods have been delivered and, if needed, installed (s.22(3)). Shorter for goods expected to perish sooner (s.22(4)). The clock pauses while a repair or replacement you asked for or agreed to is under way (s.22(6)).
  • After that: repair or replacement, then a price reduction or final right to reject (s.19(3)-(4), ss.23-24).
  • A fault that appears within 6 months of delivery is taken to have been there at delivery, unless the retailer shows otherwise or this is incompatible with the goods or the fault (s.19(14)-(15)). This presumption applies to repair, replacement, price reduction and final rejection, not to the 30-day right.
  • Time limit for a claim in England and Wales: generally 6 years from when the cause of action accrued (Limitation Act 1980 s.5). Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own limitation rules: check them before relying on this figure.

A manufacturer's guarantee (for example "3 years") is extra; it does not replace these rights against the retailer.
