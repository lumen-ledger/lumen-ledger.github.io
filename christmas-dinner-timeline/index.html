How it works

  1. Start = serving time minus cooking minutes minus resting (or holding) minutes. Out / done = serving time minus resting minutes. These are the same formulas as the "Menu" sheet of the Christmas Dinner Planner spreadsheet.
  2. Oven clash: two oven dishes clash when they need different temperatures and their cooking windows overlap. One home oven cannot do both, so the usual fixes are: cook one earlier and reheat it, move one to the stovetop or slow cooker, or bake it while the roast rests.
  3. °F or °C: pick the scale your recipes use. The clash check only compares the numbers you type, so keep every dish on the same scale (and on the same oven setting, conventional or fan, as written in each recipe).
  4. Make-ahead dishes have no time and are listed separately, so they are not forgotten.
  5. The minutes are yours: cooking times depend on weight, shape, oven and recipe, so this page does not invent them. For a turkey, the turkey thaw and roast planner uses published roasting tables by weight.
  6. Nothing is sent anywhere. "Copy link to this plan" puts the menu after the # of the address, which browsers do not send to the server: open the link on another device to see the same plan.

Safe minimum internal temperatures

The timeline tells you when to start; a food thermometer tells you when it is done. From the U.S. CDC page "Preventing Food Poisoning" (dated November 24, 2025, read 2026-11-07):

FoodMinimum internal temperature
Whole cuts of beef, veal, lamb and pork, including fresh ham145 °F (63 °C), then let it rest 3 minutes
Ground meats such as beef and pork160 °F (71 °C)
All poultry, including ground chicken and turkey165 °F (74 °C)
Fish with fins145 °F (63 °C)
Leftovers and casseroles165 °F (74 °C)

Pre-cooked ham: follow the package label. Celsius values are rounded conversions. Source: cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention. General information, not professional food-safety advice.

After dinner: leftovers

The clock does not stop at the table. From the same CDC page (dated November 24, 2025, read 2026-11-14):

  • "Never leave perishable food out for more than 2 hours (or 1 hour if exposed to temperatures above 90°F)."
  • "Refrigerate perishable food (meat, seafood, dairy, cut fruit, some vegetables, and cooked leftovers) within 2 hours."
  • "Package warm or hot food into several clean, shallow containers and then refrigerate." Small portions chill faster.
  • "Keep your refrigerator at 40°F or below and your freezer at 0°F or below."
  • Reheat leftovers and casseroles to 165 °F (74 °C) (table above).

A cautious way to use the timeline: count the 2 hours from the "Take out / done" time of each dish, not from when you sit down. In the example menu the beef roast comes out at 1:40 PM, so counted that way it goes in the fridge by 3:40 PM, even though dinner starts at 2 PM. Source: cdc.gov/food-safety/prevention. FAQ

When should I put the roast in the oven for Christmas dinner?

Work back from the time you sit down: start = serving time minus cooking minutes minus resting minutes. A roast that cooks 150 minutes and rests 20 goes in 170 minutes before you eat, so 11:10 AM for a 2 PM dinner.

Does the timeline include oven preheating?

No. Add your oven's preheat time before the first oven start, or include it in the cooking minutes of the first oven dish.

What if two dishes need the oven at different temperatures?

One home oven cannot do both at once. The usual fixes are: cook one earlier and reheat it, move one to the stovetop or slow cooker, or bake it while the roast rests. The calculator flags these clashes in red.

What internal temperature should a beef or pork roast reach?

145 °F (63 °C) for whole cuts of beef, veal, lamb and pork, including fresh ham, then let it rest 3 minutes (CDC, Preventing Food Poisoning, dated November 24, 2025).

What internal temperature is safe for turkey and other poultry?

165 °F (74 °C) for all poultry, including ground chicken and turkey. Leftovers and casseroles: also 165 °F (74 °C).

Can I enter oven temperatures in Celsius?

Yes. Pick °C next to the serving time; the example menu switches to 180 and 220 °C and a shared plan link keeps the scale. Use the temperatures from your recipes, all on the same scale and oven setting (conventional or fan), because the clash check compares the numbers you type.

How long can Christmas dinner leftovers sit out?

No more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90 °F, according to the CDC page Preventing Food Poisoning (dated November 24, 2025): refrigerate cooked leftovers within 2 hours, in shallow containers, in a fridge at 40 °F or below. A cautious approach is to count from when each dish comes out of the oven: in the example menu the beef roast is out at 1:40 PM, so it goes in the fridge by 3:40 PM.
