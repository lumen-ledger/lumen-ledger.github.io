What does it cost to run a heater (or any appliance) in the UK?

Cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × your unit rate. At the Ofgem price cap average of 32.69p per kWh, a 2,000 W heater on full power costs 65.38p per hour.

General information, not financial or safety advice. Rates: Ofgem, energy price cap 1 January to 31 March 2027, average for Direct Debit customers in England, Scotland and Wales (published 2026-11-25, read 2026-11-27): electricity 32.69p/kWh with no VAT (the government removed VAT from electricity bills from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027), gas 10.96p/kWh including 5% VAT. Your rate depends on region, payment method and tariff: use the unit rate on your bill. The cap for April–June 2027 is due by 23 February 2027.

Example wattages (check the rating plate):

Share of time at full power: 100% is the worst case. Appliances with a thermostat (heaters, fridges, ovens) switch their element on and off, so they use less than rating × hours. A plug-in energy monitor measures the real figure for any appliance with a 13 A plug.

Why the formula works

  • The wattage is on the rating plate (underneath or on the back). If only amps are given: watts = volts × amps; UK mains is nominally 230 V.
  • kWh per day = watts × hours per day ÷ 1,000. Cost = kWh × unit rate. The rate is in pence per kWh: divide by 100 for pounds.
  • The standing charge (54.77p a day for electricity on the average Direct Debit cap) is paid regardless of use, so it is left out of the cost of any single appliance.
  • Every resistance heater turns 1 kWh of electricity into 1 kWh of heat. Two 2,000 W heaters on full power cost the same per hour, whatever the type (fan, convector, oil-filled).

Electric heater vs gas central heating: cost per kWh of heat

At the price cap averages 1 kWh of electricity costs 32.69p and 1 kWh of gas 10.96p: 3.0 times less before boiler losses. A boiler turns only part of the gas into heat in the home; enter its efficiency to compare the cost of the same 1 kWh of heat.

The 90% default is an example, not a measured figure for any boiler: older or badly set up boilers can be lower. Not included: the electricity used by the boiler and pump, and heat lost from pipes. Heat pumps deliver more than 1 kWh of heat per kWh of electricity and are not covered by this comparison.

Your yearly bill at the price cap

The cap sets a maximum unit rate and daily standing charge, not a maximum bill. Ofgem's headline of £2,166 a year (Jan–Mar 2027, Direct Debit) is what 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas cost at those rates: electricity £1,017.16 (including £199.91 of standing charges), gas £1,149.42 (including £108.22), £2,166.58 in total, about £180.55 a month on average. Enter the yearly kWh from your bills or annual statement to get your own figure.

Defaults: Ofgem's GB average Direct Debit cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027 (electricity without VAT until 30 June 2027, gas including 5% VAT). Your region, payment method and tariff change the rates: copy them from your bill. With no gas supply, set gas to 0 kWh (its standing charge is then left out). Energy use is higher in winter, so a winter quarter usually costs more than a quarter of the yearly figure. The cap for April–June 2027 is due by 23 February 2027.

Questions

How much does it cost to run an electric heater for an hour in the UK?

A 2,000 W fan or convector heater on full power uses 2 kWh per hour. At the Ofgem price cap average of 32.69p per kWh (Direct Debit, 1 January to 31 March 2027) that is 65.38p per hour; a 3,000 W heater costs 98.07p and a 1,000 W setting 32.69p.

How much does a 2 kW heater add to the monthly bill?

Eight hours a day for 30 days on full power is 480 kWh: £156.91 at 32.69p per kWh. A thermostat that switches the element off half the time halves that figure. The standing charge (54.77p a day on average) is paid whether or not the heater is on, so it is not part of the heater's cost.

Is an electric heater cheaper than gas central heating?

Not per unit of heat at price cap averages. 1 kWh of electricity costs 32.69p and becomes 1 kWh of heat; 1 kWh of gas costs 10.96p. If your boiler turns 90% of the gas into heat, 1 kWh of heat from gas costs 12.18p, so electric resistance heat costs about 2.7 times as much. A plug-in heater lowers the total bill only if it lets you turn the central heating down by more than that difference, for example by heating one room instead of the whole house.

How much does an electric blanket cost to run?

A 100 W electric blanket uses 0.1 kWh per hour: about 3.3p at 32.69p per kWh. Eight hours a night for 30 nights is 24 kWh, £7.85.

How much does it cost to boil a kettle?

A 3,000 W kettle running for 3 minutes uses 0.15 kWh: about 4.9p at 32.69p per kWh. Boiling only the water you need shortens the time and the cost in proportion.

Why is my unit rate different from 32.69p?

32.69p is Ofgem's average for Direct Debit customers across England, Scotland and Wales on a standard variable tariff. Rates differ by region, payment method and tariff (fixed deals, Economy 7, prepayment). Use the unit rate printed on your bill; Ofgem's site lists the cap by region. Northern Ireland is not covered by the Ofgem cap.

How much do Christmas lights cost to run in the UK?

Cost = total watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × unit rate. At 32.69p per kWh, every 100 W of lights on for 6 hours a day over 40 days (24 kWh) costs £7.85; a 500 W display costs £39.23 over the same period. Use the watts printed on each set and the unit rate on your bill.

What does the £2,166 typical bill mean?

It is Ofgem's figure for a year at the Jan–Mar 2027 cap rates for a household on Direct Debit using electricity and gas. 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas a year reproduce it: £817.25 plus £199.91 of standing charges for electricity, £1,041.20 plus £108.22 for gas, £2,166.58 in total. The cap limits the unit rates and standing charges, not the total bill: using more energy costs more.

Does the next price cap change these figures?

Ofgem publishes the cap for 1 April to 30 June 2027 by 23 February 2027. Until then the figures here use the 1 January to 31 March 2027 cap. Enter the new unit rate in the calculator: every result scales in proportion to it.
