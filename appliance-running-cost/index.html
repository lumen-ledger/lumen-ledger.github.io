What does it cost to run a space heater (or any appliance)?

Cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × your price per kWh. At the U.S. average residential rate of 18.19 cents, a 1,500 W heater on high costs $0.27 per hour.

General information, not financial or safety advice. Rates: U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.B, average residential price January–August 2026 (release 2026-10-23, read 2026-10-31). Your own bill can differ: the average includes fixed charges and taxes, and your rate may vary by month or by usage tier. Use the price per kWh from your bill if you have it (how to find it).

Calculator

Typical nameplate watts:

Share of time at full power: 100% is the worst case. A heater with a thermostat switches its element on and off once the room is warm, so it uses less than nameplate × hours; how much less depends on the room, the insulation and the setting. A plug-in usage monitor measures it directly for any 120-volt device.

Why the formula works

  • The wattage is on the nameplate (bottom or back of the appliance). If only amps are listed: watts = volts × amps; most U.S. outlets are 120 V.
  • kWh per day = watts × hours used per day ÷ 1,000. Cost = kWh × your price per kWh.
  • The nameplate is the maximum. Appliances that cycle (refrigerators, anything with a thermostat) run at that power only part of the time; for a refrigerator, the Virginia Tech guide suggests dividing the plugged-in time by three as a rough estimate.
  • A 1,500 W electric heater turns its 1.5 kWh per hour into about 5,118 Btu of heat per hour (1 kWh = 3,412 Btu). Two 1,500 W resistance heaters on full power draw the same energy per hour, whatever the brand.

Space heater vs gas furnace: cost per unit of heat

Every electric resistance heater turns 1 kWh into 3,412 Btu. A gas furnace turns 1 cubic foot of gas (about 1,036 Btu) into heat at its AFUE efficiency: federal minimum 80% for non-weatherized gas furnaces made from 2015-11-19, 95% for those made from 2028-12-18 (10 CFR 430.32(e)(1)). Comparing the cost of the same 100,000 Btu (1 therm) of heat delivered:

At U.S. averages (electricity 18.19¢/kWh, gas $15.34/Mcf in 2025) 1 therm of heat costs $5.33 from a space heater and $1.85 from an 80% furnace ($1.56 at 95%): electric resistance heat is 2.9× as expensive. A space heater lowers your total bill only if, for every Btu it supplies, the furnace delivers more than 2.9 Btu less (for example because you turn the thermostat down and heat one room instead of the whole house). Whether that happens depends on your home; this page does not estimate it.

Not included: the furnace blower's electricity, heat lost from ducts outside the heated space, and fixed monthly charges (EIA averages include them, so the per-unit price on your bill may be lower). Heat pumps deliver more than 3,412 Btu per kWh and are not covered here.

Before you rely on a space heater

The U.S. Fire Administration (page last reviewed 2023-05-04) advises keeping anything that can burn at least 3 feet from heat sources including space heaters, and turning space heaters off when leaving the room or going to bed. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for the outlet and cords.
