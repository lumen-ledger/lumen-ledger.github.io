Texas Electricity Facts Label (EFL), explained

The EFL shows a plan's price at exactly three usage levels. Your bill depends on your usage every month. Here is how to read the label, and a calculator that prices a plan on your own year.

General information, not legal or financial advice. Rule text: 16 Texas Administrative Code §25.475 (Public Utility Commission of Texas), as last amended effective January 6, 2022, read on 2026-10-31. The example plans below are hypothetical: real plans and delivery charges differ. Always check the current EFL of the plan you choose.

What the rule requires on the label

ItemWhat §25.475 says
Average priceIn cents per kWh, at 500, 1,000 and 2,000 kWh per month for residential customers (§25.475(g)(2)).
What it includes (fixed rate plans)All recurring charges, excluding state and local sales taxes and the gross receipts tax reimbursement (§25.475(g)(2)). A recurring charge is one expected on every bill (§25.475(b)(9)).
Variable price plansThe label shows the average price for the first billing cycle only (§25.475(g)(2)). For residential customers a variable price plan can only be month-to-month (§25.475(b)(11)).
FeesEvery fee the retail electric provider (REP) may charge, and whether each one is included in the average price (§25.475(g)(3)).
ContractLength of term, minimum term and any early termination fee (§25.475(g)(4)).
Where to find itA REP that sells residential plans on its website must display the EFL prominently without asking for more than a ZIP code and the type of offer (§25.475(c)(4)(A)).

Fixed, variable, indexed: what can change

  • Fixed rate: term of at least three months, same price for the whole term. The price may move only for listed reasons, such as actual changes in the delivery (TDU) charges, ERCOT or Texas Regional Entity fees, or certain legal and regulatory changes (§25.475(b)(5)).
  • Variable price: the price can change by a method the REP decides (§25.475(b)(11)). The EFL number tells you only about the first month.
  • Indexed: the price follows a pre-defined formula based on publicly available indices (§25.475(b)(6)).

Why the three prices can mislead

Many plans add a bill credit when usage falls inside a range, or a fee when it falls below a threshold. The EFL is correct at 500, 1,000 and 2,000 kWh, but it says nothing about 999 kWh. Two hypothetical plans with the same delivery charges ($4.39/month + 5.60¢/kWh):

Plan A (simple)Plan B (bill credit)
Energy charge9.4¢/kWh14.9¢/kWh
Base charge$9.95/monthnone
Bill creditnone$100 when usage is 1,000-2,000 kWh
EFL price at 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 kWh17.9 / 16.4 / 15.7¢21.4 / 10.9 / 15.7¢
Year below (13,500 kWh)$2,197.08 (16.27¢)$2,220.18 (16.45¢)

Plan B looks a third cheaper at 1,000 kWh, yet costs $23.10 more over this year, because the credit applies in only 6 of the 12 months. At 999 kWh Plan B costs $209.18; at 1,000 kWh it costs $109.39. Monthly usage used: 1,150, 950, 800, 720, 900, 1,350, 1,700, 1,750, 1,400, 950, 780, 1,050 kWh. With a different usage pattern the winner can flip, which is why your own numbers matter.

Before you sign: checklist

  1. Plan type: fixed, variable or indexed. With variable, the EFL price is only the first month.
  2. Run your 12 months through the calculator. Look at the month-by-month result, not only the total: a credit missed in a mild month can mean a much higher bill.
  3. Read the fee list (§25.475(g)(3)) and the early termination fee (§25.475(g)(4)).
  4. Moving? The rule bars an early termination fee caused by relocation if you give a forwarding address and any evidence the REP requires (§25.475(c)(2)(C)).
  5. Contract end: for fixed rate plans the REP must send at least three written expiration notices in the last third of the term (§25.475(e)(1)(A)), and no termination penalty may apply in the 14 days before the expiration date (§25.475(e)(2)(C)(ii)).
  6. Changed your mind after switching? You can rescind without fee within three federal business days after receiving the terms of service (§25.475(f)(4)(A)(i)).
